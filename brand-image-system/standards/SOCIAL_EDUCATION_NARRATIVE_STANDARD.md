# Social Education Narrative Standard

Version: `social-education-narrative.v1`  
Updated: 2026-08-27

Use this standard before visual prompting whenever an infographic, carousel, document post, or educational social asset must earn attention and teach. It prevents the most common Infogenius failure: a beautifully rendered taxonomy that names things without giving the reader a reason to care, swipe, remember, or share.

## Core rule

Do not begin with the component list. Begin with the audience's current mental model, the tension that makes it incomplete, and the new capability the reader gains by changing it.

Every series must be reducible to one spoken promise:

> After this carousel, the reader can explain **what changes**, **why it matters**, and **what to do or watch next**.

If the promise is only “the reader will know the parts,” the narrative is not ready.

## The five-beat social lesson

For a five-slide carousel, assign one non-overlapping job to each slide:

1. **Promise** — Name a specific payoff or provocative question. The cover creates an information gap without making a false claim.
2. **Contradiction** — Surface the default belief, hidden cost, mistake, trade-off, or surprising consequence.
3. **Reframe** — Replace the old frame with the smallest useful model. Prefer two or three chunks.
4. **Reference** — Deliver the saveable map, comparison, process, checklist, or capability table.
5. **Resolution** — Synthesize the lesson into a causal loop, decision, implication, or next action.

Longer carousels may expand the proof beat, but they must preserve the same arc. Never repeat the same slide grammar six times and call it a story.

## Slide contract

Before writing any image prompt, define these six fields for every slide:

| Field | Required answer |
| --- | --- |
| Reader question | What question exists in the reader's mind at this point? |
| New information | What can the reader say after the slide that they could not say before? |
| Tension | What contrast, consequence, or trade-off makes the information matter? |
| Evidence status | Is the claim sourced fact, reasoned inference, scenario, or editorial synthesis? |
| Visual proof | What relationship can be shown physically instead of restated in prose? |
| Exit impulse | Why should the reader swipe, save, share, comment, or act? |

Reject a slide when its only new information is a noun plus a definition.

## Hook standard

A strong hook is specific, truthful, and incomplete in a productive way. Use one of these mechanisms:

- **Myth / reality**: challenge a common simplification.
- **Visible cost**: show what the default approach sacrifices.
- **Contradiction**: place two true ideas in tension.
- **Before / after**: make the transformation concrete.
- **Underestimated lever**: reveal a capability the audience overlooks.
- **Decision pressure**: make a consequential choice legible.

Avoid vague superlatives, generic “ultimate guide” language, fear without evidence, and claims that depend on withholding essential context.

## Writing rules for marketers and educators

- Write for retelling. A reader should be able to repeat the core model without reopening the slide.
- Pair every technical noun with a consequence: not “world models,” but “world models add foresight.”
- Prefer one memorable contrast over four neutral definitions.
- Use plain language in the headline and technical precision in the evidence layer.
- Keep one dominant idea per slide. Supporting labels must prove or operationalize that idea.
- Give the reference slide screenshot value: a compact map, matrix, sequence, or checklist that remains useful after the first read.
- End with synthesis, not summary. The last slide must change what the earlier parts mean together.
- Use a real conversation prompt for the caption CTA. Ask for judgment, experience, or a choice—not “thoughts?”

## Evidence language

Bold communication does not remove evidence boundaries.

- **Fact**: source it and preserve the claim's actual scope.
- **Inference**: identify it as an inference from named evidence.
- **Scenario**: state the assumptions that make it plausible.
- **Editorial synthesis**: say that the framework is authored, not scientific or market consensus.

Do not turn a teaching taxonomy into a prediction of inevitability. Do not imply vendor ranking or endorsement through visual hierarchy.

## Visual grammar rule

The visual must perform the argument.

- Contradiction → asymmetric comparison, rupture, reveal, or before/after transformation.
- Reframe → nested arenas, a small hierarchy, or a bounded map.
- Reference → matrix, weighted relationship map, annotated anatomy, or tightly controlled modules.
- Resolution → causal flow, flywheel, decision tree, or evidence-gated loop.

Prompt the function of every object. “Six glowing machines” is decoration. “Six materially different instruments, each physically embodying a distinct capability and connected to one governed bus” is semantic art direction.

## Native imagegen copy budget

Default per slide:

- One headline.
- One thesis or payoff.
- Three to seven concise labels.
- No body paragraph.
- No decorative microcopy.

Longer reference slides are allowed only when every string has a large physical label surface and the final phone crop is inspected. Exact text errors must be corrected through targeted native image edits, never code-painted over the raster.

## Narrative quality gate

Score each item 0, 1, or 2 before generation:

1. **Specific promise** — The audience knows what they will gain.
2. **Tension** — The series contains a meaningful contrast, cost, or surprise.
3. **Progression** — Every slide adds a new cognitive step.
4. **Retellability** — The framework can be repeated from memory.
5. **Payoff** — The final slide changes the meaning of the parts and invites a real next action.

Minimum gate: **8/10**. A score below 8 returns to writing; image generation cannot rescue it.

## Three-second and no-tap tests

Inspect the actual 4:5 export at approximately 360 CSS pixels wide.

- Three seconds: headline, focal relationship, and emotional direction are clear.
- No tap: the core lesson is understandable without zooming.
- Ten seconds: labels prove the headline rather than introducing a second story.
- After closing: the viewer can recall the framework in one sentence.

## Definition of done

- Spoken promise exists.
- Every slide has a declared reader question and exit impulse.
- Narrative score is at least 8/10.
- Evidence status is explicit for every public claim.
- Slide grammars differ when their semantic jobs differ.
- Reference slide has save value.
- Final slide delivers synthesis and a real CTA.
- Native exports pass exact-text, crop, and phone-read inspection.
