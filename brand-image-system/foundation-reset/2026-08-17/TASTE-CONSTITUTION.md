# Taste constitution

This is the common quality foundation for FrankX, GenCreator, Starlight Intelligence Systems, Arcanea, and future brands. It governs how decisions are made; it does not make the brands look alike.

## The central principle

Design must express a specific point of view about the brand, not a generic idea of “premium AI.” The work should feel authored, culturally aware, materially considered, and appropriate to its audience before it feels polished.

## Human authority

- Explicit founder preference outranks a rubric, agent consensus, or automated score.
- A founder rejection is a restart, not an invitation to explain the work more persuasively.
- The reason for rejection is logged as training evidence for the next round.
- The maker never gives final approval to its own work.

## Language and capitalization

- Use sentence case for headlines, labels, navigation, buttons, covers, and diagrams.
- Do not apply `text-transform: uppercase` to brand or marketing copy.
- Preserve established acronyms such as SIS, AI, API, and URL.
- Avoid techno-label language when ordinary language is clearer: “Proof” instead of “evidence rail,” “In review” instead of “validation state,” and “Contents” instead of “artifact inventory.”
- Microcopy should sound written, not generated by a control panel.

## Typography

- Typography is selected from the brand idea and tested with real language. It is not chosen from a generic “modern font” list.
- Begin every territory in black and white. If the idea cannot survive without gradients, glow, texture, or imagery, it is not ready.
- Show real specimens: brand name, product titles, a two-line statement, long-form paragraph, numbers, navigation, and a mobile crop.
- Use one distinctive voice and one highly readable support voice only when the concept requires both.
- Monospaced type is functional, not a shorthand for intelligence. Reserve it for code or aligned data that genuinely benefits from it.
- Poppins, Outfit, Inter, JetBrains Mono, and similar defaults are not automatically approved. They may survive only if a comparative specimen proves they are the best choice.
- Do not purchase or embed a commercial font until its trial specimen has passed the founder gate and licensing has been approved.

## Color and material

- Dark mode is a context, not a brand strategy.
- No brand starts from black, cyan, purple, emerald, or gold merely because it concerns AI, systems, or worlds.
- Establish hierarchy in monochrome, then assign color a semantic and emotional job.
- Glass, blur, grain, glow, metal, paper, photography, and 3D are materials to choose deliberately. None is mandatory.
- A palette must work in one color, on paper, and in low-cost production before special effects are added.

## Composition

- Prefer one memorable relationship between type, image, and space over a collection of decorative components.
- Do not use status pills, HUD chrome, card grids, node networks, orbit diagrams, signal lines, or tiny technical labels as brand decoration.
- Functional product UI may use tables, states, chips, or code where the user task requires them. Marketing should not cosplay as product telemetry.
- Each brand needs its own density, whitespace, image scale, edge behavior, rhythm, and relationship to the page.
- Real product proof is valuable, but proof does not need to dominate every cover. Some offers need editorial persuasion, story, character, or emotion.

## Logos

- A wordmark may be enough. Do not force every brand to have an abstract symbol.
- Build in vector and one color first. Test at 16px, 32px, navigation, social avatar, cover, and print.
- Reject generic initials, orbit marks, sparkles, portals, node symbols, gradient loops, and symbols whose only rationale is “connection” or “intelligence.”
- Generated logo images are exploration only. A render cannot rescue a weak mark.
- “Already used in a repo” is not evidence that a logo deserves to survive the reset.

## Imagery and generation

- Define the brand's relationship to people, places, objects, documents, illustration, and abstraction before choosing a model.
- Image generation begins only after a territory is selected and a reference set is approved.
- Generate the world or subject; compose exact typography, logos, diagrams, and public claims deterministically.
- Run a controlled pairwise benchmark with the same brief. Keep the best result regardless of model prestige.
- Generated output must not become a substitute for commissioning, photography, illustration, or real product capture when those are the correct media.

## Permanent exclusions

- “AI luxury” as a style.
- Black-glass command centers as a shared house look.
- Purple-blue gradient fog, cyan neon, gold-on-black prestige coding, and glowing orbs as defaults.
- Uppercase eyebrows, uppercase footer rails, status pills, and monospaced metadata as cover chrome.
- Generic dark dashboards, fake interfaces, abstract agent swarms, and orbit/node diagrams.
- One template recolored across brands or products.
- Automatic glass, grain, spotlight, hover-scale, motion, or 3D requirements.
- Self-awarded scores used as proof of taste.

## Cross-brand recognition gate

Every application must pass all four questions:

1. Would the brand still be recognizable if its logo were removed?
2. Could this layout, type system, palette, or image treatment be moved to another Starlight brand with only a color swap?
3. Does the work communicate the brand's emotional job before it communicates “technology”?
4. Is there one element a person could describe from memory the next day?

If question 1, 3, or 4 is no, or question 2 is yes, restart the direction.
