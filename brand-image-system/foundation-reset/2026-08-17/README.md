# Visual foundation reset

Status: active reset; nothing in the rejected cover batch is approved for publication.

## Why the previous system failed

The previous work improved production mechanics without improving taste. It made exact files, clean exports, model receipts, and readable layouts, but it inherited a narrow aesthetic from the governing files: dark canvases, glass, grids, cyan or gold accents, monospaced annotations, status pills, uppercase microcopy, and technical diagrams. That grammar was then applied across unrelated brands.

The resulting system was coherent in the least useful way: every brand looked like a variation of the same AI dashboard.

The 29/30 self-score was a false positive. It measured legibility, provenance, and completion more reliably than it measured authorship, emotional truth, distinctiveness, or founder preference. Human rejection supersedes that score.

## Reset decisions

1. Sentence case is the default. All-caps styling is prohibited in brand and marketing work, except established acronyms such as SIS.
2. There is no shared Starlight visual aesthetic. The brands may share operational rigor, but not colors, fonts, layouts, materials, or image tropes.
3. Black glass, command-center chrome, neon signals, nodes, constellations, portals, pills, and mono readouts are no longer defaults.
4. Existing logos and fonts are provisional inputs, not sacred assets. “Canonical” means locatable, not good.
5. Logos are rebuilt vector-first. Image models can support exploration and applications, never final identity proof.
6. Typography and composition are approved in black and white before color, imagery, motion, or product-cover production.
7. The maker cannot approve its own taste. Automated checks are hygiene gates; a founder no is an immediate restart.
8. No broad asset fanout happens before one approved application quartet proves the identity.

## New sequence

1. Brand strategy cards: audience, tension, promise, proof, personality, and category avoidance.
2. Two genuinely different visual territories per brand, first shown as type-led black-and-white compositions.
3. Typeface specimens and wordmark studies using real product names and real copy.
4. One application quartet per brand: web hero, product cover, social frame, and document/product surface.
5. Founder selection, rejection, or synthesis with explicit reasons logged.
6. Logo system, color, imagery, motion, and production rules built only around the selected territory.
7. A controlled model benchmark for campaign imagery after the identity system is stable.
8. Product-family expansion only after cross-brand separation and crop tests pass.

## Reference principle

The strongest identity systems begin with a strategic idea and build bespoke behavior from it. Pentagram's Intrinsic system turns “prescience” into a generative behavior and type system; Cohere uses the tension between natural and artificial to build a family of tools; Sister derives type and line behavior from Manchester's technical history; Digital Turbine removes generic complexity before adding a distinctive signal; COLLINS reframed Atera around a mission rather than a feature list.

These are references for method, not visual templates:
