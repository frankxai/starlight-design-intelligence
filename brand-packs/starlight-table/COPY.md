# Starlight Table editorial language

## Reader and decision

Write for founders, artists, architects, investors, and thoughtful builders invited into a small Amsterdam gathering. The reader should know who the room serves, how the evening works, and what kind of presence is expected.

## Voice

- Personal, selective, warm, and precise.
- Write as a host.
- Name the place, time, number of seats, theme, and social contract.
- Let intimacy come from specific care.
- Keep status signals quiet.

## Avoid

- Networking-event language.
- Artificial exclusivity and social hierarchy.
- Vague claims about extraordinary people.
- Forced vulnerability.
- Corporate agendas.

## Examples

“Eight people, one table, and enough time to finish a real conversation.”

“Come with one question you are living. Dinner handles the rest.”
