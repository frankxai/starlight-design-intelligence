# Starlight Intelligence Design

Starlight should feel like a future institution that already deserves to exist. Its design combines scientific clarity, human warmth, and a sense of scale that reaches from one person's work to cities, oceans, laboratories, and the stars.

## Visual idea

The core visual idea is **intelligence becoming possibility**. Signals connect. Knowledge acquires shape. People discover a path. A distant horizon becomes something that can be approached.

Celestial imagery is part of the brand's emotional world. It should be used with authorship and purpose. A generic starfield communicates very little. A human standing inside a living intelligence map, a city shaped by regenerative systems, or a research path extending toward a planetary horizon can carry the full idea.

## Surface modes

### Horizon mode

Use cinematic scale, strong contrast, luminous atmosphere, expressive photography or illustration, and generous composition. Pair extraordinary scenes with precise language and a clear human point of view.

Preferred subjects include people building with advanced tools, research environments, future cities, ecological systems, shared tables, deep oceans, observatories, spacecraft, and visualizations of collective intelligence.

### Operator mode

Use stable navigation, disciplined density, inspectable system state, and restrained signal color. Dashboards should feel quietly powerful. The interface reveals what is happening, why it matters, and which decision belongs to the operator.

### Academy mode

Use brighter surfaces, visible progress, beautiful knowledge maps, mentor presence, student artifacts, and a clear sense of growing mastery. The design should feel premium without turning learning into a game-show interface.

### Cultural mode

Allow richer color, character, fashion, music imagery, ritual, and expressive typography. Cultural work can be cinematic and intimate. It should remain recognizably Starlight through light, intelligence patterns, human presence, and the larger horizon.

## Composition

- Establish one visual subject and one governing idea per frame.
- Use scale deliberately. Alternate intimate human detail with civilizational perspective.
- Let whitespace create confidence on institutional pages.
- Use dense information only where the user benefits from comparison or control.
- Combine real product states with authored visual worlds instead of replacing one with the other.
- Give diagrams, evidence, research, and contribution records a clear visual hierarchy.

## Typography

Typography should feel contemporary, literate, and technically credible. Display type may carry elegance or scale in Horizon mode. Product and documentation typography must remain highly readable. Decorative science-fiction fonts are reserved for small cultural details and never carry essential information.

## Color

The base system should support deep night, clean light, and a controlled luminous spectrum. Cyan and teal can signal intelligence and connection. Violet can signal imagination and frontier work. Warm gold can mark human contribution, care, or arrival. Signal colors retain consistent operational meaning in products.

Exact tokens belong in the machine-readable brand pack. This document defines their behavior:

- dark surfaces create depth without crushing detail;
- light surfaces feel like an institution, laboratory, or studio rather than a blank SaaS template;
- luminous accents guide attention and reveal relationships;
- gradients describe energy, distance, or transition and never serve as filler;
- accessibility contrast holds across every surface mode.

## Imagery

Preferred imagery has a human or environmental subject, an authored point of view, and a reason to exist in the story. Use original generation, commissioned work, licensed photography, product capture, and diagrams with recorded provenance.

Avoid anonymous robots at laptops, floating glass cards, generic astronauts, random neon cities, decorative brains, and stock images whose only message is “technology.”

## Interface components

Operator surfaces may use timelines, graphs, inspector panes, status chips, command bars, contribution records, and provenance paths. Horizon surfaces may use full-bleed scenes, editorial chapters, interactive maps, research frontiers, and carefully staged transitions. Academy surfaces prioritize skill maps, artifacts, feedback, mentor dialogue, and progress narratives.

## Release test

A Starlight release should answer three visual questions:

1. Does this feel unmistakably connected to the larger Starlight world?
2. Does the chosen surface mode serve the person's actual task?
3. Is there enough authored detail and craft that a competent team could not replace the work with a template in an afternoon?
